## Floor 0: The Stairwell (workshop/floor-0.md)
Prepare your development environment: clone the workshop repo, install uv and Python, install the dependencies and activate the virtual environment. Everything else in this workshop runs from there.
**SYSTEM DESIGN, LAYER 0: THE PREMISE.** Every LitRPG starts with where the System came from and why it is doing this to you. Ours is one AI. The System AI built the dungeon, runs the floors and owns the dice, the rules and the memory. The flood broke it. The broadcast relay and the collars survived, so the System AI can still push canned announcements through them, but it cannot answer, roll a fair die, quote a rule or remember who did what. Each floor of this workshop rebuilds one more subsystem of the System AI, and Barnaby gets each piece first through the collar. The full map is in [The System](world/the-system.md#what-each-floor-adds-to-the-system).

## Floor 8: Production (AgentCore) (workshop/floor-8.md)
Take your game to production, one brick at a time: the dice server, the Character Agent, the Rules Agent and finally the Narrator move to [Amazon Bedrock AgentCore](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/agentcore/). Each section of this floor moves one brick to the cloud while the others keep running on your machine, so the game keeps working at every step.
**SYSTEM DESIGN, LAYER 8: A PERSISTENT WORLD.** A LitRPG world exists whether or not the reader is looking. On your laptop, the System ends when you close the terminal. On AgentCore, the dice, the registry, the rules and the Narrator each run on their own, the characters live in AgentCore Memory, the rules in a managed knowledge base, and every turn leaves a trace. That is the difference between a game you run and a world that runs: Cordula's sheet is still there tomorrow, and so is the accountant's grudge. See [The System](world/the-system.md#what-each-floor-adds-to-the-system).

## The Crawl Handbook (workshop/world/crawl-handbook/)
### Ability Scores and Modifiers

Every Crawler has six ability scores: Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom and Charisma. A new Crawler rolls each score with 4d6 drop lowest: four six-sided dice, keep the three highest, add the three dice. The System AI rolls the six scores with roll_dice (faces 6, count 4, drop_lowest 1) and announces them. A score never assigned or rolled defaults to 10.

The ability modifier is the score minus 10, divided by 2, rounded down.

| Score | Modifier |
|---|---|
| 3 | -4 |
| 4 to 5 | -3 |
| 6 to 7 | -2 |
| 8 to 9 | -1 |
| 10 to 11 | +0 |
| 12 to 13 | +1 |
| 14 to 15 | +2 |
| 16 to 17 | +3 |
| 18 to 19 | +4 |
| 20 | +5 |

What each ability does in the crawl:

- Strength: Power Move Calls, lifting, carrying, holding a door shut.
- Dexterity: Footwork Move Calls, the Final Beat, stealth, balance, throwing objects (and fish).
- Constitution: Endurance Move Calls and Groove.
- Intelligence: Choreography Move Calls, locks, puzzles, the System AI's fine print.
- Wisdom: Read the Room Move Calls, noticing hidden opponents, traps and ambushes.
- Charisma: Presence Move Calls, sponsors, crowds, talking an opponent out of a dance-off.

### Ability Checks

When a Crawler tries an action whose outcome is uncertain, the System AI calls for an ability check and announces it: roll 1d20, add the matching ability modifier, and compare the total to a Difficulty Class (DC). A total that meets or beats the DC succeeds.

| Task | DC |
|---|---|
| Easy (step across a slick tide-pool tile) | 10 |
| Moderate (pick a keycard reader, climb a bull kelp stipe) | 13 |
| Hard (cross the Underground in the dark without a sound) | 16 |
| Very hard (out-argue a Bellevue lawyer) | 20 |

Dexterity checks cover any action that needs agility, reflexes, balance or quiet: sneaking, tumbling, keeping footing on a swaying houseboat, slipping out of a grab, throwing Chonks accurately. Strength checks cover lifting, pushing and holding. Constitution checks cover holding breath and pushing through exhaustion. Intelligence checks cover locks, puzzles and recall. Wisdom checks cover noticing and reading people. Charisma checks cover persuading, bluffing and performing.

Advantage: when the situation favors the Crawler, roll two d20 and keep the higher (roll_dice faces 20, count 2, drop_lowest 1). Disadvantage: roll two d20 and keep the lower.

Help: a party member who is not acting can help with a check and grants the Crawler advantage, if the helper could plausibly assist.

### Dance-Offs

Conflict in the crawl is settled by a dance-off, never by violence. Opponents are people the System AI dropped into the dungeon; the opponents want to win, not to hurt anyone. Barnaby fights and dances beside Cordula: he takes Choreography and Presence calls, she takes Power, Footwork and the Final Beat.

Procedure:

1. Tempo. The System AI sets the tempo and announces it; the tempo sets the opponent's base bonus: Steady (opponent adds +0), Driving (+2) or Frantic (+4).
2. Move Calls. A dance-off has three rounds. Each round the System AI decides a Move Call and announces it; the Move Call names an ability: Power (Strength), Footwork (Dexterity), Endurance (Constitution), Choreography (Intelligence), Read the Room (Wisdom) or Presence (Charisma). The System AI never calls the same move twice in a row.
3. Rolls. The Crawler rolls 1d20 plus the called ability modifier. The opponent rolls 1d20 plus the opponent's own ability modifier plus the tempo bonus. Every roll goes through roll_dice.
4. Assists. Each round, one party member who is not dancing may assist: +2 to the dancer's roll, or use one ability that fits the Move Call (an Ink Cloud during Footwork, an Eight-Point Hold that keeps an opponent from answering the Move Call).
5. Round result. The higher total wins the round. The loser loses Groove equal to the difference, at least 1 and at most 6. A tied round goes to the Final Beat.
6. The winner is whoever takes two of three rounds. If the rounds end level, the Final Beat decides.

Winning a dance-off: the opponent leaves the floor, often relieved, the party gains Hype, and the System AI may drop a loot box. Losing: the party loses Groove and the System AI announces the loss with relish.

Statuses that change dance-off rolls:

- Off-Beat: -4 to rolls while it lasts (Ink Cloud: two rounds; Suction Latch: until the latch lets go; other causes: the next roll). Caused by Ink Cloud, Suction Latch, a slick floor or a well-timed heckle.
- Held: cannot answer the next Move Call; the round goes to the other side. Caused by Eight-Point Hold or Siphon Bind.
- Soggy: -2 to Presence. Cordula is immune.

### The Final Beat

The tie-breaker. When a round or a dance-off ends in a tie, the System AI's drum speeds up and plays trick beats. The dancer who stops exactly on the final strike wins.

Procedure: each side rolls 1d20 plus their Dexterity modifier. The target is exactly 20. The total closest to 20 without going over wins. A total over 20 means the dancer kept moving past the final strike and loses. If both totals pass 20, or both land on the same total, the drum plays again and both sides roll again.

The Final Beat is the only roll in the crawl where a high total can lose. The System AI finds that reversal delightful.

The Final Beat borrows one structural idea from the modern powwow circuit, where judges break a tie by speeding up the drum until a dancer misses the final beat. The crawl credits the powwow circuit for that tie-break and does not depict powwow dances; Crawlers dance their own styles.

### Groove, Mana, Benched and Cancelled

Groove is how long a Crawler can stay on the dance floor. Mana is how many abilities a Crawler can use before resting. Groove and Mana are derived stats: the Character Agent computes them when a Crawler is created, and nobody chooses either value.

- Groove = 10 + twice the Constitution modifier + level, never less than 4.
- Mana = 5 + the Intelligence modifier + the Wisdom modifier, never less than 1.

A Crawler regains all Groove and Mana by resting in a Safe Room.

Benched: at 0 Groove, a Crawler leaves the dance floor. The Crawler wakes up in the last Safe Room visited with 1 Groove, loses the loot box from that dance-off, and loses half of the Crawler's Hype, rounded down. Nobody dies in the crawl.

Cancelled: a Crawler benched three times on the same floor has the segment cancelled for the rest of the floor. The Crawler rides the elevator down to the next floor with no loot from the floor the Crawler left.

Companions such as Shelly and Chonks have their own Groove. A benched companion returns to the companion's Companion Egg and binds again at the next Safe Room.

### Stealth and Avoiding a Dance-Off

The party does not have to dance every encounter. A party can try to slip past opponents unseen.

Procedure: the party's sneakiest member rolls a Dexterity check, 1d20 plus that member's Dexterity modifier, against a DC of 10 plus the highest Wisdom modifier among the opponents. If the party is under Chromatophore Stealth, the roll has advantage. Success: the party passes unseen and gets no dance-off, no Hype and no loot. Failure: the dance-off starts with the opponents ahead: the tempo goes up one step.

Three more ways around a dance-off:

- Persuasion: a Charisma check against DC 13 (DC 16 for anyone in Bellevue) talks an opponent out of the dance-off. Developer Advocates can always be distracted by a question about the Developer Advocate's favorite feature.
- Locks: Barnaby's Arm-Lock Pick opens a door or a shortcut instead of dancing through the room that guards the door or shortcut.
- Environment: Shelly's Saltwater Jet can push opponents out of a corridor, and an Ink Cloud can cover a retreat.

Stealth is never a way to hurt anyone. Stealth puts the party somewhere else.

### Hype, Sponsors and Loot Boxes

Hype is a hidden stat the System AI tracks: how much the audience loves a Crawler. The System AI shows a Crawler's Hype whenever the System AI chooses.

- +1 Hype for each dance-off round won.
- +3 Hype for winning a Final Beat.
- +5 Hype for any achievement.
- Half of all Hype is lost when a Crawler is benched.

Sponsors watch Hype. At 10, 25 and 50 Hype, a sponsor may send a loot box, and the System AI delivers it with a sponsor message. Sponsors include the Benthic Collective, a Ballard fishing-and-kombucha cooperative that adores Barnaby; the Hydros-9 Aquatic Collective, a venture-funded South Lake Union water-tech startup on its ninth pivot, which also adores Barnaby; the Bellevue Consortium, famous for the Doors '94 operating system, which sponsors the opponents and resents every upset; and Cadabra, Inc., whose delivery drones drop every loot box and whose weaponized obstacles wait on deeper floors.

Loot boxes come in four tiers:

- Bronze: common gear, a potion, a Ferry Token.
- Silver: useful gear or a companion upgrade.
- Gold: rare gear, a skill book or a Companion Egg. A Companion Egg binds one wild animal already living on that floor; it does not hatch a new animal.
- Desperation: awarded for winning a dance-off with 3 Groove or less. Always useful, always humiliating.

The System AI writes every loot description, and each description mocks the Crawler's choices gently. The System AI never mocks who the Crawler is.

### Titles, Skills and Fusion

Titles are passive bonuses the System AI awards for remarkable or ridiculous accomplishments. A Crawler may hold multiple titles; the System AI announces each title.

- Salish Sea Guardian: +15% movement on wet or slippery terrain; immune to water-based environmental debuffs. Cordula's starting title.
- First Final Beat: +1 to the holder's next three Final Beat rolls.
- Ferry Line Survivor: patience; advantage on Endurance calls while the holder is outnumbered.

Achievements, skill books from loot boxes, and trainers in Safe Rooms unlock skills; practice also works: a skill used ten times in dance-offs gains a level.

Fusion: two Level 3 skills held by members of the same party fuse at a Safe Room forge into one skill. Example: Ink Cloud (Barnaby) and Saltwater Jet (Shelly) fuse into Inkjet, a long-range blinding spray.

Exploits: the System AI respects a combination the System AI did not see coming, and pays out a loot box for that combination the first time.

### Safe Rooms, Ferry Tokens and Floors

Safe Rooms: cedar-walled rooms with a fire and rain on big windows, in the Northwest Regional style. No dancer can start a dance-off inside a Safe Room. Resting in a Safe Room restores all Groove and Mana. Most Safe Rooms have a shop; some have a forge for skill fusion. Every Safe Room has a terminal on the broadcast relay: the System AI's public library, where the party reads the Strands manual, course videos and code samples.

Ferry Tokens: the crawl's currency. Dancers spend Ferry Tokens in Safe Room shops on potions, repairs and gear. Safe Room shops also accept item trades.

Potions available on the Ship Canal floor:

- Ballard Fresh-Hop Haze: +20% Mana regeneration for one floor; any dancer who drinks Ballard Fresh-Hop Haze during a dance-off is Off-Beat for that round.
- Ship Canal Lager: cures Soggy.
- Locks Porter: +2 Constitution for one floor.

Floors: the dungeon has eight floors under Puget Sound, with an optional deeper floor. Each floor has stairs down, at least one Safe Room and a boss. The floors are Pier 59 Collapse, the Eelgrass Nursery, the Bull Kelp Cathedral, the Ship Canal, the Estuary, the Underground, the Bluffs, Bellevue and the Narrows, and ALMOCO, the Corrosion Cathedral (optional, Floor 9).

Terrain:

- Wet floor: Footwork rolls at -1 unless the dancer is immune (Salish Sea Guardian, Harbor Seal).
- Swaying floor (houseboats, kelp bulbs): Read the Room DC +2.
- Tide floor (the Estuary): the dance floor floods every third round; any dancer or item not anchored is swept off.
