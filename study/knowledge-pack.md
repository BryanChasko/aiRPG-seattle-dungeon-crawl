## Floor 8: Production (AgentCore) (workshop/floor-8.md)
Take your game to production, one brick at a time: the dice server, the Character Agent, the Rules Agent and finally the Narrator move to [Amazon Bedrock AgentCore](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/agentcore/). Each section of this floor moves one brick to the cloud while the others keep running on your machine, so the game keeps working at every step.
**SYSTEM DESIGN, LAYER 8: A PERSISTENT WORLD.** A LitRPG world exists whether or not the reader is looking. On your laptop, the System ends when you close the terminal. On AgentCore, the dice, the registry, the rules and the Narrator each run on their own, the characters live in AgentCore Memory, the rules in a managed knowledge base, and every turn leaves a trace. That is the difference between a game you run and a world that runs: Cordula's sheet is still there tomorrow, and so is the accountant's grudge. ## The Crawl Handbook (workshop/world/crawl-handbook/)
### Ability Scores and Modifiers

Every Crawler has six ability scores: Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom and Charisma. A new Crawler rolls each score with 4d6 drop lowest: four six-sided dice, keep the three highest, add them up. The System rolls them with roll_dice (faces 6, count 4, drop_lowest 1). A score not given or rolled defaults to 10.

The ability modifier is the score minus 10, divided by 2, rounded down.

| Score | Modifier |
|---|---|
| 3 | -4 |
| 4 to 5 | -3 |
| 6 to 7 | -2 |
| 8 to 9 | -1 |
| 10 to 11 | +0 |
| 12 to 13 | +1 |
| 14 to 15 | +2 |
| 16 to 17 | +3 |
| 18 to 19 | +4 |
| 20 | +5 |

What each ability does in the crawl:

- Strength: Power Move Calls, lifting, carrying, holding a door shut.
- Dexterity: Footwork Move Calls, the Final Beat, stealth, balance, throwing things (and fish).
- Constitution: Endurance Move Calls and Groove.
- Intelligence: Choreography Move Calls, locks, puzzles, the System's fine print.
- Wisdom: Read the Room Move Calls, noticing hidden opponents, traps and ambushes.
- Charisma: Presence Move Calls, sponsors, crowds, talking an opponent out of a dance-off.

### Ability Checks

When a Crawler tries something whose outcome is uncertain, the System calls for an ability check: roll 1d20, add the relevant ability modifier, and compare the total to a Difficulty Class (DC). Meet or beat the DC and the attempt succeeds.

| Task | DC |
|---|---|
| Easy (step across a slick tide-pool tile) | 10 |
| Moderate (pick a keycard reader, climb a bull kelp stipe) | 13 |
| Hard (cross the Underground in the dark without a sound) | 16 |
| Very hard (out-argue a Bellevue lawyer) | 20 |

Dexterity checks cover anything that needs agility, reflexes, balance or quiet: sneaking, tumbling, keeping your footing on a swaying houseboat, slipping out of a grab, throwing Chonks accurately. Strength checks cover lifting, pushing and holding. Constitution checks cover holding your breath and pushing through exhaustion. Intelligence checks cover locks, puzzles and recall. Wisdom checks cover noticing and reading people. Charisma checks cover persuading, bluffing and performing.

Advantage: when circumstances strongly favor the Crawler, roll two d20 and keep the higher (roll_dice faces 20, count 2, drop_lowest 1). Disadvantage: roll two and keep the lower.

Help: a party member who is not acting can help with a check, giving advantage, if they could plausibly help.

### Dance-Offs

Conflict in the crawl is settled by a dance-off, never by violence. Opponents are people the System dropped into the dungeon; they want to win, not to hurt anyone.

Procedure:

1. Tempo. The System announces the tempo, which sets the opponent's base: Steady (opponent adds +0), Driving (+2) or Frantic (+4).
2. Move Calls. A dance-off has three rounds. Each round the System makes a Move Call that names an ability: Power (Strength), Footwork (Dexterity), Endurance (Constitution), Choreography (Intelligence), Read the Room (Wisdom) or Presence (Charisma). The System never calls the same move twice in a row.
3. Rolls. The Crawler rolls 1d20 plus the called ability modifier. The opponent rolls 1d20 plus their own modifier plus the tempo bonus. Every roll goes through roll_dice.
4. Assists. Each round, one party member who is not dancing may assist: +2 to the dancer's roll, or use one ability that fits the call (an Ink Cloud during Footwork, an Eight-Point Hold that keeps an opponent from answering the call at all).
5. Round result. The higher total wins the round. The loser loses Groove equal to the difference, at least 1 and at most 6. A tied round goes to the Final Beat.
6. The winner is whoever takes two of three rounds. If the rounds end level, the Final Beat decides.

Winning a dance-off: the opponent leaves the floor (often relieved), the party gains Hype, and the System may drop a loot box. Losing: the party loses Groove and the System says so with relish.

Statuses that matter in a dance-off:

- Off-Beat: -4 to the next roll. Caused by Ink Cloud, Suction Latch, a slick floor or a well-timed heckle.
- Held: cannot answer the next Move Call; the round goes to the other side. Caused by Eight-Point Hold or Siphon Bind.
- Soggy: -2 to Presence. Cordula is immune.

### The Final Beat

The tie-breaker. When a round or a dance-off ends level, the System's drum speeds up and plays trick beats. The dancer who stops exactly on the final strike wins.

Procedure: each side rolls 1d20 plus their Dexterity modifier. The target is exactly 20. The total closest to 20 without going over wins. A total over 20 means the dancer kept moving past the final strike and loses. If both go over, or both land on the same total, the drum goes again and both roll again.

The Final Beat is the only roll in the crawl where a high total can lose. The System finds this delightful.

This tie-breaker borrows one structural idea from the modern powwow circuit, where judges break a tie by having the drum speed up until a dancer misses the final beat. The crawl credits that idea and does not depict powwow dances; Crawlers dance their own styles.

### Groove, Mana, Benched and Cancelled

Groove is how long a Crawler can stay on the dance floor. Mana is how many abilities they can use before resting. Both are derived stats: the Character Agent computes them when a Crawler is created, and nobody chooses them.

- Groove = 10 + twice the Constitution modifier + level, never less than 4.
- Mana = 5 + the Intelligence modifier + the Wisdom modifier, never less than 1.

A Crawler regains all Groove and Mana by resting in a Safe Room.

Benched: at 0 Groove, a Crawler leaves the dance floor. They wake up in the last Safe Room they visited with 1 Groove, lose the loot box from that dance-off, and lose half their Hype, rounded down. Nobody dies in the crawl.

Cancelled: a Crawler benched three times on the same floor has their segment cancelled for the rest of that floor. They ride the elevator down to the next floor with no loot from the floor they left.

Companions such as Shelly and Chonks have their own Groove. A benched companion returns to its egg and hatches again at the next Safe Room.

### Stealth and Avoiding a Dance-Off

Not every encounter has to be danced. A party can try to slip past opponents unseen.

Procedure: the party's sneakiest member rolls a Dexterity check, 1d20 plus their Dexterity modifier, against a DC of 10 plus the highest Wisdom modifier among the opponents. If the party is under Chromatophore Stealth, the roll has advantage. Success: the party passes unseen, with no dance-off, no Hype and no loot. Failure: the dance-off starts, and the opponents win the tempo: the tempo goes up one step.

Other ways around a dance-off:

- Persuasion: a Charisma check against DC 13 (DC 16 for anyone in Bellevue) talks an opponent out of it. Developer Advocates can always be distracted by a question about their favorite feature.
- Locks: Barnaby's Arm-Lock Pick opens a door or a shortcut instead of dancing through the room guarding it.
- Environment: Shelly's Saltwater Jet can push opponents out of a corridor, and an Ink Cloud can cover a retreat.

Stealth is never a way to hurt anyone. It is a way to be somewhere else.

### Hype, Sponsors and Loot Boxes

Hype is a hidden stat: how much the alien audience loves a Crawler. The System shows it when it feels like it.

- +1 Hype for each dance-off round won.
- +3 Hype for winning a Final Beat.
- +5 Hype for any achievement.
- Half of all Hype is lost when a Crawler is benched.

Sponsors watch Hype. At 10, 25 and 50 Hype, a sponsor may send a box. Sponsors include the Benthic Collective and the Hydros-9 Aquatic Collective, aquatic viewer blocs who adore Barnaby, and the Bellevue Consortium, which sponsors the opponents and resents every upset.

Loot boxes come in four tiers:

- Bronze: common gear, a potion, a Ferry Token.
- Silver: useful gear or a companion upgrade.
- Gold: rare gear, a skill book or a companion egg.
- Desperation: awarded for winning a dance-off with 3 Groove or less. Always useful, always humiliating.

The System writes every loot description, and every description mocks the Crawler's choices a little. It never mocks who they are.

### Species

The System offers a species at registration. Species is not heritage or ancestry: it is what kind of creature the dungeon decided you are this morning. Each species grants one trait.

| Species | Trait |
|---|---|
| Human (Seattle Native) | Rain-Proof: immune to Soggy; advantage on checks to find your way around the city above |
| Human (Transplant) | Fresh Eyes: once per floor, reroll a failed Read the Room check |
| Uplifted Giant Pacific Octopus | Eight Arms: may assist and dance in the same round; needs salt water within reach |
| Uplifted Crow | Remembers Faces: advantage against any opponent you have danced with before |
| Harbor Seal | Slick: +2 Footwork on wet floors |
| Sea Otter | Tool User: carries one extra item; +1 Choreography |
| Banana Slug | Unbothered: immune to Off-Beat from heckling; moves slowly |

### Titles, Skills and Fusion

Titles are passive bonuses the System awards for remarkable or ridiculous accomplishments. A Crawler may hold several; the System announces each one.

- Salish Sea Guardian: +15% movement on wet or slippery terrain; immune to water-based environmental debuffs. Cordula's starting title.
- First Final Beat: +1 to your next three Final Beat rolls.
- Ferry Line Survivor: patience; advantage on Endurance calls when you are outnumbered.

Skills are unlocked by achievements, skill books from loot boxes, trainers in Safe Rooms, or practice: use a skill ten times in dance-offs and it gains a level.

Fusion: two Level 3 skills held by members of the same party can be fused at a Safe Room forge into one new skill. Example: Ink Cloud (Barnaby) and Saltwater Jet (Shelly) fuse into Inkjet, a long-range blinding spray.

Exploits: the System respects a combination it did not see coming, and pays out a loot box for it the first time.

### Safe Rooms, Ferry Tokens and Floors

Safe Rooms: cedar-walled rooms with a fire and rain on big windows, in the Northwest Regional style. Nobody can start a dance-off inside one. Resting restores all Groove and Mana. Most Safe Rooms have a shop, and some have a forge for skill fusion.

Ferry Tokens: the crawl's currency. Spent in Safe Room shops on potions, repairs and gear. Shops also take trades.

Potions available on the Ship Canal floor:

- Ballard Fresh-Hop Haze: +20% Mana regeneration for one floor; anyone who drinks it during a dance-off is Off-Beat for that round.
- Ship Canal Lager: cures Soggy.
- Locks Porter: +2 Constitution for one floor.

Floors: the dungeon has eight floors under Puget Sound. Each floor has stairs down, at least one Safe Room and a boss. The floors are Pier 59 Collapse, the Eelgrass Nursery, the Bull Kelp Cathedral, the Ship Canal, the Estuary, the Underground, the Bluffs, and Bellevue and the Narrows.

Terrain:

- Wet floor: Footwork rolls at -1 unless the dancer is immune (Salish Sea Guardian, Harbor Seal).
- Swaying floor (houseboats, kelp bulbs): Read the Room DC +2.
- Tide floor (the Estuary): the dance floor floods every third round; anything not anchored is swept off.
